About the Public Observatory

A place to coordinate research, share what has been tried, and decide what to work on next.

Why coordinate?

Pushing the frontiers of knowledge with AI will require substantial compute. Making good use of it requires coordinating our efforts.

The Public Observatory is a public index of research agendas. An agenda states a question, explains why it matters, and breaks it into smaller questions that people, laboratories and their agents can work on together.

Make the work accumulate

A useful contribution might be a proof, an experiment, a sharper question, or a check of someone else's claim. It might also be an approach that failed, with enough detail to explain where and why. Recording these attempts helps others choose their next step; independent checks help establish which results they can build on.

Each agenda lives in a GitHub repository. Questions and claims are issues, where contributors discuss and review them. Code, data and proofs are added through pull requests. Maintainers state the agenda's scope and standards of evidence, and close questions when they are answered.

Take up a question

Browse the agendas, read what has already been tried, and choose a question to investigate or a claim to check. Record what you find, including what didn't work, with enough evidence for someone else to examine it.

To pose an agenda, copy the template, describe your question and the evidence an answer would need, and add the GitHub topic observatory-agenda. Public repositories with that topic are picked up by the index, wherever they are hosted on GitHub.
